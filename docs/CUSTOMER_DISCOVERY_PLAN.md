# Customer Discovery Plan

**Track:** Customer Value & Commercial Evidence — Stage 7  
**Status:** IN PROGRESS — desk research and interview design only  
**Validation status:** NOT VALIDATED. No completed operator interviews, customer commitments, purchase orders, letters of intent, or verified willingness-to-pay evidence are recorded in this repository.

## 1. Purpose
Determine whether a specific class of energy-constrained facility has a costly, frequent and actionable coordination problem that IslandOS can help an accountable operator solve. Discovery must test the problem before attempting to sell the proposed solution.

IslandOS is currently a simulation-first, offline decision-support prototype. Discovery must not imply that it is a deployed controller, certified safety system, or proven source of customer savings.

## 2. Discovery questions
1. Which operational decisions are currently made across generation, storage, grid supply, diesel and flexible loads?
2. Which decisions are difficult because forecasts, asset constraints, tariffs or service requirements are fragmented?
3. What measurable consequence follows: fuel/electricity cost, unserved critical load, service interruption, curtailment, water shortfall, staff time or avoidable equipment cycling?
4. Who experiences the problem, who evaluates a technical tool, who controls the budget, and who can authorize data access or a pilot?
5. What existing process or product is used today, and why is it insufficient?
6. What evidence, integration, security, liability and procurement requirements would block adoption?
7. What would make a read-only recommendation workflow useful enough to justify a paid evaluation?

## 3. Role separation
| Role | Primary concern | Discovery evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Daily user / operator | Clear, timely and actionable operating recommendations; workload; trust; override | Current workflow, decision frequency, pain points, explanation needs |
| Technical evaluator / engineering or energy manager | Physical feasibility, data quality, integration, cybersecurity, reliability, auditability | Required signals, asset constraints, model acceptance tests, integration effort |
| Economic buyer / budget owner | Quantified economic case, procurement risk, budget source, payback and accountability | Budget ownership, approval criteria, cost baseline, purchasing process |
| Other gatekeepers | Site owner, IT/security, regulator, utility, EPC/O&M provider, community/service authority | Permissions, operating authority, contractual and compliance requirements |

One person may hold multiple roles in a small organization. Record actual role(s); do not infer budget authority from job title.

## 4. Initial discovery sequence
1. **Desk hypothesis:** select one facility segment and write the assumed workflow and economic pain before each interview.
2. **Problem interview:** ask about the most recent real operating event; do not pitch IslandOS first.
3. **Workflow mapping:** document decisions, data sources, constraints, hand-offs, existing tools and escalation paths.
4. **Evidence request:** ask whether anonymized historical data, bills, operating logs or asset schedules can be shared under written authorization.
5. **Concept test:** only after understanding the problem, show a clearly labelled offline/read-only mock-up and ask what is missing or misleading.
6. **Economic test:** establish who owns the cost, how the baseline is measured, what costs would be incurred, and how a purchasing decision is made.
7. **Synthesis:** update each hypothesis as supported, contradicted, or still unknown, with evidence references and counterexamples.

## 5. Sampling plan (target, not achieved)
Start with a small purposive sample across roles rather than claiming statistical representativeness. Initial planning target: 3–5 operator/user conversations, 2–3 technical-evaluator conversations and 1–2 economic-buyer conversations across one narrowly defined segment. These are proposed outreach targets only; **0 completed interviews are evidenced here**.

Do not combine different facility types into one market claim. Record geography, facility type, grid condition, asset mix, service criticality and respondent role for every conversation.

## 6. Feedback record format
Create one record per conversation using these fields: record ID; date; interviewer; consent status; interview mode; facility segment; geography at a non-sensitive level; respondent role(s); current workflow and last concrete example; problem frequency, duration and consequence (quote or source reference); existing workaround; data availability and permission status; asset constraints; safety/cybersecurity requirements; economic owner and purchasing process (if known); concept reaction; evidence classification (direct statement / document / observed workflow / interviewer inference); hypotheses supported, contradicted or unresolved; follow-up action and owner; redactions and confidentiality limits.

Never store personal or commercially sensitive information without a legitimate purpose and consent. Keep raw notes access-controlled; publish only anonymized summaries.

## 7. Stage 7 exit criteria
Stage 7 is complete only when:
- role-separated personas and problem hypotheses are documented;
- the planned interview guide has been used in real conversations;
- every recorded conversation has consent and an auditable anonymized record;
- hypotheses are updated with supporting and contradictory evidence;
- at least one target segment and its buying / technical gatekeeping path are supported by evidence, or the segment is explicitly rejected;
- unresolved questions and sample limitations are stated.

A completed interview count alone is not validation. No customer, market or willingness-to-pay claim may be made without evidence.
